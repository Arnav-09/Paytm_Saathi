# Health insurance basics (India)

## Sum insured
The sum insured is the maximum amount the insurer will pay in a policy year. Once it is used up, further claims are not paid unless the policy offers restoration or a top-up. Family floater policies share one sum insured among all members.

## Co-payment and deductible
Co-payment is a fixed percentage of every admissible claim that you pay yourself (for example 10%). A deductible is a fixed rupee amount you pay first before the insurer pays anything. Policies differ on whether co-payment is applied before or after the deductible, so check the policy wording.

## Room rent limit and proportionate deduction
Many policies cap the room rent per day (for example Rs. 5,000) or at a percentage of the sum insured. If you take a costlier room, the insurer may reduce other related charges, such as doctor fees, surgery and nursing, in the same proportion. This is called proportionate deduction. Medicines, tests and consumables are often not reduced, but this depends on the policy. Choosing a room within the limit avoids this surprise.

## Waiting periods
An initial waiting period (often 30 days) applies after a new policy starts, except for accidents. Pre-existing diseases usually have a waiting period of up to 3 years under most policies, and named conditions such as cataract, hernia or joint replacement often have a 1 to 4 year wait. Maternity cover typically needs 9 months to 4 years of continuous cover. Waiting periods count from the first policy start date, and they carry over on a portability or renewal without a break.

## Pre-existing diseases
These are conditions diagnosed or treated before you bought the policy. They must be disclosed honestly on the proposal form. Hiding them can lead to claim rejection. After the waiting period, they are covered. Under IRDAI rules, the insurer's right to reject a claim for non-disclosure is limited after a continuous period (the moratorium, now 5 years under current IRDAI rules; check the latest circular).

## Cashless and reimbursement claims
Cashless: at a network hospital, the insurer pays the hospital directly after pre-authorisation. You inform the insurer in advance for planned admissions (usually 48 hours before) or within 24 hours for emergencies. Reimbursement: you pay first and then submit the bills, discharge summary and claim form, usually within 15 to 30 days of discharge.

## Common exclusions
Typical exclusions include cosmetic surgery (unless after an accident), dental treatment (unless accidental), weight loss surgery, infertility treatment, injuries from hazardous sports, and treatment outside the policy's coverage area. Always read the exclusions list and the sub-limits.

## Sub-limits
Some treatments have a lower cap than the sum insured, such as Rs. 40,000 per eye for cataract or a fixed amount for maternity. The sub-limit applies even if the sum insured is higher.

## Free-look and portability
After buying a policy there is a free-look period (at least 15 days, and many insurers now give 30 days) in which you can cancel for a refund minus small charges. You can port your policy to another insurer at renewal, and the earned waiting-period credit moves with you, subject to the new insurer's underwriting.

## Non-payable items
Some charges are commonly not paid, such as registration, admission fees, attendant meals, telephone and TV charges, toiletries, and administrative or service charges. IRDAI has been reducing the list of non-payable items; the policy wording and the latest IRDAI guidance decide what applies.
