# Payments, UPI safety and KYC (India)

## You never need a PIN to receive money
A UPI PIN is needed only to send money or to check a balance. If anyone asks you to enter your UPI PIN to "receive" money, a refund or a prize, it is a scam. Likewise, a "collect request" that you did not expect should be declined.

## Never share OTP, PIN, CVV or passwords
Banks, wallets, insurers, RBI and the police never ask for your OTP, PIN, CVV, card number or password, by phone, SMS, WhatsApp or email. Do not install screen-sharing apps such as AnyDesk or TeamViewer at the request of a caller who claims to be support.

## Common scams
Fake KYC-expiry messages with a link; fake customer care numbers found by searching online; fake refund or cashback links; electricity or SIM disconnection threats; wrong-transfer calls that ask you to send the money back; lottery and prize messages; fake job or investment groups; and fake loan apps. Check the link carefully, and open your bank or wallet only through its official app.

## Money debited but the payment failed
Most failed transactions are reversed automatically. Under RBI's turnaround-time framework, a failed UPI payment is generally reversed within one working day after the transaction day, and delays can earn compensation to the customer. Wait a day, note the transaction ID or UTR, then complain to the app and, if needed, to the bank's nodal officer and the RBI Ombudsman.

## Money sent to the wrong person
Report quickly with the transaction ID. The bank can request the recipient's bank to help, but the recipient must agree to return it unless a fraud is established. Never send more money to "release" the first payment.

## Reporting suspicious calls and messages
Report fraud on the national helpline 1930 and at cybercrime.gov.in. Suspected fraudulent calls and messages can also be reported on the Sanchar Saathi portal (sancharsaathi.gov.in) using the Chakshu facility.

## KYC: what it is
KYC (Know Your Customer) is identity verification required by RBI so that banks and wallets know who their customers are. Minimum KYC accounts, such as basic wallets, have low balance and transaction limits. Full KYC, done with Aadhaar OTP or e-KYC, video KYC or an in-person check, unlocks higher limits and more services. Limits are set by RBI and each provider and change over time, so confirm them in the app.

## KYC documents
Commonly accepted officially valid documents include Aadhaar, passport, voter ID, driving licence and NREGA job card. PAN is needed for many financial products. Your name should match across documents. Share documents only inside the official app or at a branch, and never send photos of Aadhaar, PAN or cards to unknown people.

## Updating KYC
Banks may ask you to update KYC periodically. They do this through their official app, branch or registered email, not through random links. If a message creates panic about your account being blocked today, verify with the bank directly.
