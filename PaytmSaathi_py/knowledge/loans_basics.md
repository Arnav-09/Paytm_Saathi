# Loans basics (India)

## EMI
EMI is the fixed monthly instalment of a loan. Each EMI has an interest part and a principal part. In the early years most of the EMI is interest. A longer tenure lowers the EMI but increases the total interest paid. A shorter tenure does the opposite.

## Interest rate: fixed and floating
A fixed rate stays the same for an agreed period. A floating rate moves with a benchmark, so your EMI or tenure can change. Compare loans by the total cost, not only by the rate or the EMI.

## Processing fee and other charges
Lenders charge a one-time processing fee (commonly 0.5% to 3% of the loan) that is often deducted before the money is paid out. There may also be documentation charges, insurance premiums bundled into the loan, late-payment penalties and foreclosure or prepayment charges. Ask for the Key Fact Statement (KFS), which RBI requires lenders to give, showing the APR (annual percentage rate) and all charges in one place.

## Prepayment and foreclosure
Paying part of the loan early, or closing it fully (foreclosure), reduces interest. Under RBI rules, lenders cannot charge prepayment or foreclosure charges on floating-rate loans taken by individuals for non-business purposes. Fixed-rate loans and business loans may carry charges, so check the agreement.

## Credit score
A credit score between 300 and 900 reflects repayment history. Scores of about 750 and above usually get the best rates and approvals. Pay every EMI and card bill on time, keep card usage below roughly 30% of the limit, avoid many loan applications in a short time, and check your credit report for errors at least once a year (you can get a free report every year from each credit bureau).

## FOIR and eligibility
Lenders look at how much of your monthly income already goes to EMIs (FOIR, fixed obligations to income ratio). Many lenders prefer total EMIs under about 40% to 55% of income, depending on the loan type and income. A higher income, fewer existing EMIs, a higher credit score, a longer tenure or a co-applicant can raise the amount you qualify for.

## Documents
Typically: PAN, Aadhaar, recent photographs, 3 to 6 months of bank statements and income proof (salary slips for salaried people; ITR, GST or business proof for self-employed). Secured loans need property, vehicle or gold documents.

## Warning signs of loan scams
Be careful if someone asks for an advance fee, registration fee or insurance fee to release a loan; offers a loan without any checks; pushes you to install an app and give access to contacts or photos; or threatens you. Use only lenders registered with RBI, and check the Digital Lending Apps list on the RBI website. Genuine lenders do not ask you to pay to receive a loan.

## Recovery and harassment
Lenders and their agents must follow RBI's fair-practice rules. Harassment, abuse or contacting your family and friends to shame you is not allowed. Complain to the lender's grievance officer, then to the RBI Ombudsman, and to the police or cybercrime.gov.in for threats.
